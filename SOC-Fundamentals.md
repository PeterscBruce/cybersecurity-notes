# SOC Fundamentals

Organisations hold huge amounts of confidential data — disruption to this can cause massive damage. Security practices must be constantly updated and SOC teams operate 24/7.

**Main Priority: Detection and Response**

---

## Detection

**Vulnerabilities**
- Weaknesses an attacker can exploit e.g. outdated OS or software
- Unfixed vulnerabilities can cause catastrophic damage

**Unauthorised Activity**
- Attacker gaining access to credentials

**Policy Violations**
- Sending confidential files insecurely

---

## Response

**Incident Response**
- Minimise impact of the incident
- Perform root cause analysis

---

## Three Pillars of SOC

People, Process, Technology

---

## People

Automation handles a lot but humans are always needed to filter false positives and identify real threats. Without people a SOC just chases noise.

**SOC Team Roles**

**CISO** — Top of the chain, sets security strategy and policy for the whole organisation

**SOC Manager** — Manages the SOC team day to day, reports to CISO

**Level 3 Analyst** — Most experienced, handles complex threats, threat hunting, creates detection rules

**Level 2 Analyst** — Takes escalations from Level 1, investigates deeper, responds to incidents

**Level 1 Analyst** — First line of defence, monitors alerts, triages and escalates to Level 2

---

## Alert Triage

- First response to any alert
- Analyse severity of the alert
- Answer the 5 Ws — Who, What, Where, Why, When
- Escalate with a report covering all 5 Ws

---

## Technology

**SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)**
- Collects logs from various network devices
- Identifies suspicious activity
- Uses behaviour analytics and threat intelligence

**EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response)**
- Monitors devices in real time and historically
- Works at endpoint level
- Can automate responses and allows quick investigation

**Firewall**
- Barrier between internal and external networks
- Monitors and filters traffic
- Uses detection rules to block suspicious activity before it reaches the internal network
